# Teaching notes

Why the packets are built the way they are. They come from a summer of making these at home and watching which pages kids actually finished.

## It's a game, not homework

When a page feels like a comprehension test, kids rush it. Questions that ask for an opinion ("Who impresses him? Who worries him?"), a judgment ("Was she right?") or something to make (a comic, a song, an item) get real effort. Keep pure recall to a few interesting questions a page.

## No deadline by default

Each page unlocks at a point in the story, not on a date. A fast reader and a slow reader both do the page when they get there. Write unlock points as milestones ("once the ship sets sail"), never as twists, because kids flip ahead.

## Ten minutes a page

One sheet, three or four activities. A page that takes 40 minutes doesn't get done, and the next one doesn't either.

## Progress they can see

A cover XP bar or a map they color in as they go does more than any reward chart. It's in the book's world, so it doesn't feel like school.

## Stump the grown-up

Kids love writing questions the grown-up can't answer, and a good question means knowing the book. Lose gracefully.

## Seal a prediction, open it later

A fold-over prediction early in the book, opened near the end, gets a real reaction either way. Being wrong is half the fun. Say so on the page.

## Argue the other side

The take-a-side page ends with "write the best argument against your own answer". It's the hardest line on the page and the most valuable.

## The grown-up's job is small

The stamp, a dinner question, and the guide when it's wanted. "What happened in the book today?" over dinner is the cheapest check there is. The guide is for settling arguments, and the book is always the tiebreaker.

## Honest answers

An AI-written answer guide is only as good as what the AI could check. The red box at the top says what that was. If the child disagrees with the guide, assume they're right and look it up together.

## Printing details that matter

Lines are sized to the hand: wide (34px) for 9 and under, narrower (29px) after. The 1.25in left margin lets pages go into a three-hole binder or a prong folder without punching through answers. There's a stamp, not a signature line.
